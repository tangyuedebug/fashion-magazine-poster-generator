# Fashion Poster Quality Control

## Before generation

- One communication function and one editorial proposition.
- One primary composition model and at most one supporting model.
- Two to four preserved reference anchors; a clear discard list.
- One style family, one primary tension, and one dominant focal point.
- A deliberate text field and negative-space plan.
- Dynamic title/deck/metadata derived from visible subject features, unless exact copy was supplied.
- Style family plus at least five other fields changed from the latest output.
- For fragment mode: asset inventory completed, one surface fact and one underlying proposition are explicit, and keep/drop/order decisions are intentional.

## After generation

- Does the poster read immediately as fashion editorial rather than a generic portrait?
- Is the focal point obvious and is the eye path intentional?
- Are the model's action, gaze, and expression visible and physically plausible?
- Are hands, hair, garment construction, and shadows coherent?
- Do background, wardrobe, text, and accent color form a hierarchy?
- Is the palette distinct from the latest output?
- Is the short text readable enough for the intended use?
- Does the text describe the visible style signature instead of repeating a fixed template title?
- Are title, deck, and metadata distinct in role and short enough for the image model?
- Are there no copied logos, watermarks, celebrity cues, or long gibberish text?
- If layered, do all layers share crop, scale, safe area, and light direction?
- For fragment mode, does the title add a second meaning instead of captioning the assets? Is no weak asset forced into the collage? Are screenshots, names, addresses, tickets, and original-person details protected? Does the package still read at mobile-thumbnail size?

If one important item fails, perform one targeted correction. Do not replace the entire direction unless the proposition itself failed.
